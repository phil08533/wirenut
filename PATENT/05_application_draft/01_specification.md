# SEALABLE TWIST-ON WIRE CONNECTOR WITH CAPTIVE HEAT-RECOVERABLE SLEEVE

## FIELD OF THE INVENTION

[0002] The present disclosure relates to electrical connectors, and more particularly to twist-on wire connectors provided with an integrated, heat-recoverable environmental sealing system for making moisture-resistant electrical splices, for example in outdoor low-voltage landscape lighting installations.

## BACKGROUND

[0003] Twist-on wire connectors, commonly called wire nuts, are among the most widely used devices for splicing electrical conductors. A typical twist-on connector includes a generally conical insulating shell containing a tapered helical spring. Stripped conductor ends are inserted into the open end of the shell, and the connector is rotated so that the spring draws the conductors together into a twisted, gas-tight mechanical and electrical joint. Twist-on connectors are inexpensive, fast to install, require no tools, and are familiar to virtually every electrician and homeowner.

[0004] Standard twist-on connectors, however, provide no environmental seal. The open end of the shell leaves the splice exposed to moisture. In outdoor applications — landscape lighting, irrigation systems, well pumps, exterior fixtures — moisture ingress causes corrosion of the conductors and spring, increased contact resistance, galvanic degradation, nuisance failures, and, in low-voltage lighting systems, voltage drop and flickering.

[0005] Several approaches exist for sealing such splices, each with significant shortcomings. (a) Electrical tape wrapped over the connector and wires is slow, skill-dependent, unsightly, and unwinds or admits moisture over time. (b) Dielectric grease applied into the connector cavity is messy and provides no mechanical retention of the seal. (c) Pre-filled sealant connectors, in which the shell is factory-filled with silicone gel, provide useful moisture resistance but are messy to install and re-enter, provide little or no strain relief to the conductor bundle, and the gel can be displaced during installation, particularly with multiple large conductors. (d) Heat-shrinkable tubing with an adhesive lining is highly effective for sealing linear splices such as crimped butt connectors, but is fundamentally incompatible with twist-on connectors as conventionally supplied: the tubing must be threaded onto a conductor **before** the joint is made, must then be slid over the completed joint, and a twist-on connector is substantially larger in diameter than the conductor bundle, so tubing sized to pass over the connector cannot recover tightly onto the conductors unless a very high shrink ratio is used and the tubing is correctly positioned — a multi-step, error-prone process requiring the installer to select, cut, thread, park, position, and recover a separate component. In addition, the rotation of a twist-on connector during installation tends to displace or wind up any loose sleeve placed over it beforehand.

[0006] There accordingly exists a need for a wire connector that installs with the identical, familiar motion of a standard twist-on connector — with nothing extra to select, thread, or position — and that can then be environmentally sealed in a single additional step, providing both moisture sealing and strain relief, and that is manufacturable at low cost at scale.

## SUMMARY

[0007] The present disclosure provides a sealable electrical connector assembly in which a heat-recoverable, sealant-lined sleeve is factory-installed on a twist-on wire connector in a partially recovered, captive state. A first portion of the sleeve is recovered (or otherwise retained) onto an exterior skirt of the connector shell, permanently locating the sleeve on the connector. A second portion of the sleeve extends beyond the open end of the connector in a substantially unrecovered state, defining an open entry bell. Because the bell's inner diameter substantially exceeds the conductor bundle diameter, conductors are inserted and the connector is rotated exactly as with a standard twist-on connector; the captive sleeve neither obstructs insertion nor winds up during rotation. After the twisted joint is made, the installer applies heat; the second portion recovers radially onto the conductor insulation, and the sealant lining flows into the interstices between conductors and bonds to both the shell and the conductor insulation, forming a continuous environmental seal and a strain-relieving collar in one step.

[0008] In some embodiments the connector shell includes a molded annular retention formation — for example a barb ring having an inclined lead-in surface and a transverse retention surface — dimensioned to mechanically retain the sleeve against axial displacement and against the torsional loads of rotational installation. In other embodiments the sleeve is carried by a collar engageable with the skirt of a standard connector shell, permitting the sealing system to be applied to existing connector designs. Further embodiments include a stepped-diameter sleeve reducing the required shrink ratio; a thermochromic indicator that changes appearance when full recovery temperature has been reached, giving the installer positive visual confirmation of a complete seal; elastically recoverable (cold-shrink) sleeves held expanded by a removable core; and mastic or other sealant linings.

[0009] The assembly is supplied as a unitary article. Installation requires no additional parts, no threading of tubing before splicing, and no repositioning; the only step added to conventional practice is the application of heat. Embodiments described herein are dimensioned to seal conductor combinations from two 18 AWG conductors up to three 10 AWG conductors, including dissimilar-size combinations common in landscape lighting.

## DETAILED DESCRIPTION

### First embodiment — captive pre-tacked sleeve (FIGS. 1–3)

[0018] Referring to FIGS. 1 and 2, a sealable connector assembly 10 comprises a twist-on wire connector 20 and a heat-recoverable sleeve 40 captively retained thereon. The connector 20 comprises an insulating shell 22 — for example molded polyamide — having an open end 24 communicating with an internal tapered cavity 26 in which a tapered helical spring 28 of square-section steel wire is retained. The shell 22 has a generally conical exterior narrowing from a base skirt portion 30 adjacent the open end 24 toward a closed tip, and carries external grip formations 32 (wings or ribs) by which installation torque is applied. The connector 20 may be of conventional construction and, in some embodiments, is a commercially available connector certified to applicable wire-connector standards.

[0019] The sleeve 40 is a tube of heat-recoverable, cross-linked polyolefin having an interior sealant lining 46, for example a hot-melt adhesive having an activation temperature (e.g., about 80 °C) below the full-recovery temperature of the sleeve (e.g., about 120 °C). The sleeve 40 comprises a first portion 42 and a second portion 44. The first portion 42 is partially recovered onto the skirt portion 30, gripping the shell and axially and rotationally locating the sleeve 40 on the connector 20. In this embodiment the partial recovery of the first portion 42 is performed during manufacture ("tacking"), for example by directing heated air at only the first portion while the second portion is shielded or spaced from the heat source, such that the sealant lining 46 within the first portion is not fully activated and the second portion 44 remains substantially at its expanded, as-supplied diameter.

[0020] The first portion 42 is recovered onto a substantially cylindrical band of the skirt portion 30 located below the grip formations 32, so that the grip formations remain exposed and fully usable for rotational installation. In one embodiment the first portion has an axial length of about 6 mm to about 7 mm and begins about 1 mm below the root of the grip formations 32.

[0021] The second portion 44 extends beyond the open end 24 and terminates at a distal end 48, defining an open entry bell whose inner diameter is substantially the as-supplied inner diameter of the sleeve — greater than the outer diameter of the skirt portion 30 and several times greater than the diameter of the largest rated conductor bundle. Conductors 50 having stripped ends 52 and insulation 54 are therefore insertable through the bell into the cavity 26 without contacting or displacing the sleeve, and the connector may be rotated to form the joint without the sleeve winding up, because the sleeve is fixed to the shell and rotates with it.

[0022] Referring to FIG. 3, after the twisted joint is formed, heat applied to the second portion 44 — by a lighter, torch, or hot-air tool — causes it to recover radially onto the insulation 54 of the conductors 50, forming a seal region 60 in which the activated sealant lining 46 flows into the interstices between adjacent conductors and forms a fillet 62 bonding the shell 22, the sleeve 40, and the insulation 54 into a continuous moisture barrier. The recovered second portion additionally couples bending loads on the conductor bundle into the shell rather than into the twisted joint, providing strain relief.

### Dimensional relationships

[0023] The sleeve is selected such that (a) its as-supplied inner diameter exceeds the outer diameter of the skirt portion 30, permitting factory assembly over the shell, and (b) its fully recovered inner diameter is no greater than about 85% of the diameter of the smallest conductor bundle the connector is rated to receive, ensuring residual hoop compression on the smallest bundle. For representative connector sizes these conditions correspond to a diametral shrink ratio of at least about 3:1, and in the embodiments tabulated below, about 4:1.

[0025] In embodiments, the second portion 44 is dimensioned such that, after full recovery and accounting for longitudinal shrinkage of about 10%, the sleeve extends at least about 15 mm along the conductor insulation beyond the open end 24, providing the seal region 60 with sufficient length for both sealing and strain relief.

### Second embodiment — shell with retention formation (FIG. 4)

[0026] Referring to FIG. 4, in a second embodiment the shell 22 is molded with an annular retention formation 70 on the skirt portion 30. The retention formation 70 comprises an inclined lead-in surface 72 facing away from the open end 24, over which the sleeve is slid during factory assembly, and a transverse retention surface 74 facing toward the open end, behind which the first portion 42 of the sleeve engages. In one embodiment the lead-in surface 72 is inclined at about 30° to about 60° to the shell axis and the retention surface 74 is substantially perpendicular to the shell axis, and the formation projects radially about 0.8 mm above the adjacent skirt surface. The retention formation positively resists both axial pull-off and rotational slip during installation torque, widening the manufacturing process window: with the formation 70, the first portion 42 may be recovered only lightly, or the sleeve may even be retained by the formation alone without thermal tacking. The shell of this embodiment may further include an extended skirt 76 lengthening the sealing land. The retention formation may alternatively comprise a groove, a plurality of ribs, knurling, or other surface texture performing the equivalent retaining function.

### Third embodiment — retrofit collar (FIG. 5)

[0027] Referring to FIG. 5, in a third embodiment the sleeve 40 is carried by a collar 80 having resilient engagement features 82 (for example an interference band or snap fingers) engageable with the skirt of a standard, unmodified twist-on connector 20. The collar 80 carries the retention geometry of the second embodiment, and the sleeve 40 is retained on the collar with its second portion 44 defining the entry bell as previously described. This embodiment permits the sealing system to be supplied either pre-assembled to a connector or as a separate adapter for field or factory application to existing connectors.

### Method of use (FIGS. 6A–6D)

[0028] Referring to FIGS. 6A–6D, a sealed connection is formed by: providing the assembly 10 as a unitary article; inserting stripped ends 52 of two or more conductors 50 through the open bell of the second portion 44 into the cavity 26 (FIG. 6A); rotating the connector 20 by the grip formations 32 to draw the conductors into a twisted mechanical and electrical joint, the sleeve rotating with the connector (FIG. 6B); applying heat from a heat source 110 — an open flame, hot-air tool, or other source — to the second portion 44 until it recovers onto the conductor insulation and the sealant lining activates (FIG. 6C); and allowing the assembly to cool, yielding a sealed, strain-relieved connection (FIG. 6D). Notably, steps illustrated in FIGS. 6A and 6B are identical to the installation of a conventional twist-on connector; no tubing is selected, cut, threaded, parked, or positioned by the installer.

### Further embodiments

[0029] **Stepped sleeve (FIG. 7).** The sleeve 40 may be a stepped-diameter member 100 having a smaller-diameter proximal region pre-formed to approximately the skirt diameter and a larger-diameter distal bell, reducing the shrink ratio required of the distal region and permitting use of lower-ratio, lower-cost tubing while preserving the captive, open-bell installation behavior.

[0030] **Recovery indicator (FIG. 8).** The sleeve 40 may carry a thermochromic indicator 90 — a stripe, dot, or full-surface coating — that irreversibly changes appearance upon reaching substantially the full-recovery temperature of the sleeve, providing the installer visual confirmation that sealing temperature was achieved and reducing under-heated field failures.

[0031] **Sealant variants.** The sealant lining 46 may comprise a hot-melt adhesive, a mastic band disposed within the second portion, a UV-curable or chemically activated sealant, or combinations thereof. In embodiments, the lining is confined to, or of greater thickness in, the second portion 44, so that factory tacking of the first portion does not activate sealant unnecessarily.

[0032] **Elastic (cold-shrink) variant.** The sleeve 40 may be an elastically recoverable elastomeric sleeve held in a radially expanded state by a removable core, the core extending through the second portion; withdrawal of the core after installation recovers the second portion onto the conductors without applied heat. All references herein to a "heat-recoverable" sleeve should be understood to encompass, in the alternative, such elastically recoverable sleeves except where heating is expressly recited.

[0033] **Connector variants.** While the illustrated connector is a twist-on connector with helical spring 28, the captive-sleeve sealing system may be applied to other insulation-piercing, push-in, lever-actuated, or crimp connectors having a shell with an exterior surface onto which the first portion 42 can be retained. The shell may be molded with the sleeve in place (insert molding), or the sleeve may be retained by an adhesive bead, a clamp ring, or the collar of the third embodiment.

[0034] **Materials.** In embodiments the shell 22 comprises polyamide (e.g., PA66) rated for the recovery temperature of the sleeve; the sleeve comprises cross-linked polyolefin, ultraviolet-stabilized for outdoor exposure, for example carbon-black loaded; and the sealant lining comprises an ethylene-vinyl-acetate-based hot-melt adhesive bonding to polyvinyl chloride, polyethylene, and polyamide surfaces. These materials are exemplary and non-limiting.

[0035] **Kit form.** The connector and sleeve may alternatively be supplied unassembled as a kit in a common package, the sleeve including an alignment feature cooperating with the shell to establish the captive shipped configuration described above upon assembly by the user.
